Design breakdown

The decisions behind Blackout Arena: what I designed, why, and how I built and tested it.

Core gameplay loop

  1. 01ExploreJungle paths, temples, ritual chambers
  2. 02DiscoverRelics, carved symbols, secret rooms
  3. 03FightGuardians that adapt to the player
  4. 04SurviveComplete the mission, earn the way forward
  5. 05DescendGo deeper: higher risk, darker truth

↻ Each mission repeats the loop one layer deeper, so curiosity pulls the player forward and risk pushes back.

Mystery

Every ruin hides a question: who built the arena, and why?

Threat

Enemies that pressure the player and adapt to how they play.

Trial

The arena is a test. Progress has to be earned, not given.

Atmosphere

Cinematic, fire-lit spaces that make the player feel watched.

⚔

Combat & enemy AI

Intent: fights should read clearly in third person, and enemies should feel like guardians of the ruins, not random mobs.

  • Designed the guardians' behaviour and encounter roles for third-person combat.
  • Built their decision-making with Behavior Trees + Blackboard.
  • Tuned encounter pacing and difficulty with spreadsheet-based balancing.
⛰

Level design

Intent: make curiosity the main driver. The player should always see a reason to go deeper and pay a risk cost for it.

  • Handcrafted spaces: overgrown jungle paths, ancient temples, underground ritual chambers.
  • Environmental storytelling through carved symbols, relics and secret chambers.
  • Exploration that rewards curiosity and punishes mistakes.
◎

Missions & narrative

Intent: use the mission structure to unfold the mystery one layer at a time.

  • Mission-driven single-player campaign that escalates with depth.
  • Folk Heroes lore: past challengers who left relics and warnings, and mostly never returned.
  • Cinematic beats built in Sequencer to land key story moments.
⚙

Systems & implementation

Intent: a designer who can build their own designs gets them tested faster.

  • Implemented gameplay in Blueprints and C++.
  • Built with Nanite and Lumen for dense geometry and cinematic lighting.
  • Full controller support; min-spec target of GTX 1650 / 8 GB RAM.
✎

Documentation

Intent: keep the whole team building the same game.

  • Wrote the GDD plus feature and mission specs.
  • Kept balancing data (enemy stats, difficulty) in spreadsheets so tuning stayed fast.
▲

Testing, iteration & shipping

Intent: ship it and learn from real players, instead of polishing forever.

  • Playtest → debug → iterate cycles from first public demo (early 2026) to release.
  • Used AI tools to speed up scripting, content and testing workflows.
  • Took it through Steam: store page, release and post-launch updates.

What shipping a game taught me

Ideas are the easy part. The design work is in the loop, the systems, and in cutting what doesn't serve them.

Playtests beat opinions. Watching someone play shows problems faster than any document.

Scope is a design tool. A small team ships when every feature earns its place.
